## 4. Interview talk-track for projects

Use the engineer's STAR: **Context → Constraints → Decisions → Outcomes → Learnings.**
**Spend the most time on Decisions** — interviewers grade judgment and tradeoffs, not story polish.

**Lead with the problem and the alternatives you weighed.** Restraint reads as senior thinking:
> *"I needed background jobs. I considered a real queue, but the operational overhead didn't fit a
> solo project's scale — so I used a Postgres-backed job queue and accepted lower throughput for
> simpler failure recovery."*

**Name constraints without defensiveness** (solo dev, no budget, deadline). Strong engineers state
constraints plainly; weak answers pretend they didn't exist.

### 🎯 The "do people use it?" question — your honest answer

This is the one you've been dreading. **Answer it head-on, then pivot forward.** Right now:

> *"It's built to production quality, but I haven't driven adoption yet. That's actually what I'm
> working on now — I've instrumented it with analytics and I'm running a pilot with a group of
> job-seekers I tutor, to measure whether it actually cuts their search time."*

That converts "no users" into **initiative and a concrete plan** — which is itself a signal. And once
the proof lane lands, this becomes a *much* better answer with real numbers in it.

**Keep each project answer to 60–90 seconds** and end on a concrete, TRUE result — a technical metric
or a specific learning. Never a vague "it went well." **Always close with a Learnings beat**: what
broke, what you'd rebuild. Self-critique signals judgment more than a flawless narrative.

**Map your tradeoffs onto the team's actual stack and problems.** Research the role first.
