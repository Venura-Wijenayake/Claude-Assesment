# Market Read — Sacramento, 2026

Researched 2026-08-11 with live sources (cited inline). Where a number couldn't be verified, it says so.
This is the honest version, including the parts that aren't fun.

---

## The headline: your final-two loss was the market, not you

2026 is **one of the worst entry points into software development in over a decade**:

- New grads are only **~7% of Big Tech hires**; recent-CS-grad unemployment ~**6.1%**
- Typical junior searches run **5–6 months and 200+ applications**
- Two structural causes: (1) AI absorbed the boilerplate/bug-fix work that used to justify a junior
  headcount, and (2) **layoffs put experienced mid/senior engineers into the junior applicant pool**

> When an employer chooses between a fresh grad and a laid-off engineer with 3 years of shipping
> experience *at similar pay*, they take the latter. **That is almost certainly what "a little more
> experience" meant at Superior Farms.** It is the defining feature of this market, not a personal failing.
> — [Extern, CS Job Market 2026](https://www.extern.com/post/computer-science-job-market-2026-guide)

**Strategic consequence:** treat pure junior-SWE as a **medium-term** goal, not the thing that pays
next month. That's not giving up — it's the same "pragmatic, not purist" call you already made.

## The four lanes, ranked by attainability for *you*

### 🟢 1. IT Support / Tech Ops / Desktop / Jr Sysadmin — **best immediate fit**
- **Pay:** Desktop Support avg **~$24.49/hr** ($20.77–$26.39) · Help Desk Specialist **~$26.98/hr**
  ($21.54–$30.77) · Help Desk Support Specialist I **~$29/hr** (~$54.9k) · Tier 3 / sysadmin **$30–$40+/hr**
- **Demand:** Solid and stable — state agencies, districts, healthcare, MSPs. **Least disrupted by AI**
  of the four families.
- **Why you:** You already do this work (Natomas USD, Dell Tier 1/2, district IT) + A+ Core 1 + ITIL 4.
  You just went final-two. **One or two more cycles should land it.**
- ⚠️ **Note on your $25/hr floor:** plain "desktop support" *averages below your target*. Aim at
  **Help Desk Specialist / Tier 2–3 / Tech Ops / jr sysadmin** titles, not entry desktop support.

### 🟢 2. State of California IT classifications — **the underused unlock. Read this one.**
- **Pay:** **Information Technology Associate ~$4,935–$6,614/mo** (**~$59k–$79k/yr ≈ $28–$38/hr**),
  rising to ~$6,521–$8,740/mo at higher levels.
- **Why this is the single best-fit path in this whole document:**
  **State hiring runs on a scored exam + eligibility list, and education substitutes for experience.**
  It structurally **bypasses the exact "more experience wins" dynamic that just cost you the job.**
  Your B.S. in CS satisfies the minimum qualifications on paper.
- Sacramento is a government town — the State, counties, and health systems hire these continuously.
- **Action:** work **CalCareers** listings and **take the Information Technology Associate exam.**
  This is a different *game* than private-sector interviewing, and the rules favor you.
- Bonus: state offices downtown are **light-rail accessible** — which matters (see permit note below).

### 🟡 3. Data Analyst / Reporting / Business Analyst — **best technical target**
- **Pay:** Sacramento Data Analyst **~$88k–$109k/yr** (band ~$71.6k–$118.5k) · Business Analyst
  **~$92k/yr**. **Entry/junior analysts commonly start ~$25–$35/hr** — realistic and clears your floor.
- **Demand:** Moderate and **steadier than SWE** — state government, healthcare (Sutter, Kaiser,
  UC Davis Health), finance. Good remote/hybrid availability.
- **Gap to close:** **SQL is a near-universal screen**, plus Excel and one BI tool (Power BI/Tableau).
  You have the CS baseline; you lack an analyst title and a dashboard portfolio.
- **This is your best *technical* pivot** — more attainable than SWE and it uses the degree.

### 🔴 4. Junior Software Developer — **medium-term, not now**
- **Pay (careful):** listed averages ~$49–$52/hr / ~$101k–$109k are **blended and overstate a true
  first offer** — they include people with 1–3 yrs. **Real entry offers often land $60k–$90k.**
- **Demand:** Weak for true entry-level (see headline above). Remote listings are plentiful
  (~8,700+) but draw national pools — hardest to win as a new grad.
- **Screens:** portfolio + coding assessment often screen *harder than the degree*. **A CS degree is
  table-stakes in 2026, not a differentiator.** ← this is precisely why the proof lane matters.

### Also worth targeting: **QA Analyst / Tester**
QA Analyst avg **~$90.6k** · QA Tester **~$70.1k** — both well above your floor, CS degree frequently
"desired," and lighter on the experience screen. Needs some test-design + SQL upskilling.

## ⚠️ The learner's permit is a real, fixable blocker

This deserves blunt treatment because it silently disqualifies you from your **best-fit** family:

- **California law: a learner's permit holder may *never* drive alone** — a licensed driver 25+ must
  always be in the front seat. **There is no employment exception.**
- **Consequence:** you effectively cannot self-drive to work. That's a **hard blocker for multi-site
  desktop-support / field-tech / MSP roles**, which routinely require "valid driver license and
  reliable transportation" — i.e., exactly the IT-support jobs you're otherwise strongest for.
- **Transit reality:** SacRT is usable but limited — 80+ bus routes, 43 miles of light rail, 52
  stations, ~4am–midnight weekdays, ~15-min peak / 30-min off-peak. Covers the central grid, Blue Line
  (N–S), and Gold Line (east to Folsom) fine. **Suburban office parks and MSP client sites are poorly
  served.**

**Two responses, do both:**
1. **Near-term filter:** prioritize **single-site, transit-accessible** employers (downtown state
   offices near light rail) and **remote/hybrid** roles (data analyst, QA, some help desk).
2. **Get the full California driver license.** It removes a recurring screen on the roles you're most
   likely to land. Among everything in this campaign, this is one of the **highest-ROI, fully-in-your-
   control** actions — arguably on par with the proof work.

## The plan this implies

**Barbell strategy — stop treating these as competing.**

- **Income now (weeks–months):** Tech Ops / Help Desk Tier 2–3 **+ CalCareers State IT Associate**.
  Clears $25/hr, uses the degree, and you're already competitive.
- **Pivot target (3–12 months, while employed):** **Data Analyst** — build SQL + 2–3 dashboards.
  More attainable than SWE and it's a genuine technical role.
- **Long game:** SWE, entered *sideways* — get into an org via IT/Tech Ops, build internal tooling and
  scripts (your Linux Essentials + CS degree do real work here), then pivot internally. Internal
  pivots skip the brutal external junior screen entirely.

**Contract-to-hire is a legitimate on-ramp, not a step down.** It's explicitly designed to convert,
it's accessible with non-traditional experience, and it's how you're already working (Robert Half).
~2M people work contract through US staffing agencies weekly.
