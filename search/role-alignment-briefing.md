## 4. The LeetCode / hackathon question

**LeetCode: stop entirely for 90 days. Not reduce — stop.**

LeetCode is *conversion* tooling, not *access* tooling. It turns an onsite you already have into an offer. You don't have SWE onsites; you get filtered at the resume screen, step 1, and no number of solved problems changes step 1. Meanwhile CS grads are at **6.1% unemployment**, entry-level generalist SWE postings are **~25% below the 2023 peak**, and across 165M applications benchmarked in 2026 only **~0.5% of applications become a hire**. The format has also shifted — debug-broken-code rounds, take-homes, pair programming — because AI solves easy/mediums instantly.

**Hackathons: skip.** The "60% conversion" numbers you'll find are *employer-side* metrics from pre-screened corporate hiring events, not weekend MLH-style hackathons. Individual-participant-to-offer conversion is **not published anywhere credible** — treat it as very low. One exception: an explicit **hiring hackathon** run by a named company you'd work for. That's a job application with a weird format; do that one.

**Portfolio projects: park them.** Lighthouse Open Health and Job Radar with zero users read as "can start things." Ten real users beats ten thousand lines. Finish one *later, while employed*.

**The opportunity-cost math:** you have ~15 focused hrs/week = ~180 hrs over 12 weeks. 180 hrs of LeetCode ≈ 250 problems ≈ **0 expected offers**. The same 180 hrs buys: A+ Core 2 + Security+ (~90 hrs), all three state exams filed (~4 hrs), 30 warm contacts + 4 agency relationships (~50 hrs), and 60 referral-attached applications (~36 hrs).

**Say this when someone repeats the standard advice:** *"LeetCode is for people who already have interviews. I've reached final two and a four-person panel — I don't have a format problem, I have a warm-introduction-volume problem. I'll do algorithm work when I have a loop that requires it."*

## 5. Highest-ROI thing you can do

# Get your driver's license. ~10–20 hours.

Not a certification. Still the highest-return item on this list by a wide margin, and nobody appears to have told you.

**In California, adults 18+ have no mandatory permit-holding period** — you can book the behind-the-wheel test as soon as you're ready (the 6-month wait applies to minors). You need a licensed adult 25+ for practice and a car for the test; driving schools rent both. Realistically **a few weeks of evenings.**

That is a **$12–$19/hr swing over your current $22.50** on roles you are otherwise qualified for and are currently self-filtering out of. No certification on earth expands your addressable market this much for this little. Do this in parallel with everything else.

**Runner-up (the actual credential answer): CompTIA A+ Core 2. ~40 hrs, ~$260.** Core 1 alone is worth **nothing** on a resume — A+ is awarded only when both cores are passed, and both must be from the same version series. Your Core 1 investment has a shelf life and is currently a sunk cost. Finish it within 6 weeks or lose it. A+ won't raise your ceiling; it's the floor keyword that public-sector and healthcare HR screens for by name. Security+ after that (~50–60 hrs, ~$400) — strongest single public-sector keyword because of DoD 8140/8570. **⚠️ Ignore every "average salary with cert X" figure you find; that web is exam-prep marketing.** Buy certs for access, not income.

**CCNA:** genuinely valuable, too slow for a 3-month window. Start it in month 4, employed. **AZ-900 / AWS CP:** skip. **SQL:** do it against your *employer's real data* after you're hired — that's the mechanism of the internal transfer, not a Coursera certificate.
