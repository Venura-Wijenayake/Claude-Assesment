# The Non-State Search — Plan

**11 Aug 2026 · Companion to `role-alignment-briefing.md`**

State civil service is running in the background: exams filed, eligibility banked, 12-month shelf
life. That lane is slow (2–4 months) and needs almost no ongoing effort once the exams are in.

This document is the *other* lane — everything that isn't California state government — and how to
run it so it doesn't collapse back into $22/hr help desk.

---

## 1. Why "apply at your tier" is harder outside state service

State government makes the tier explicit. There are published classifications, and a
**B.S. in Computer Science literally satisfies the minimum qualification** for IT Associate. You
either meet it or you don't. The degree does visible, mechanical work.

Nowhere else works like that. A private company has no "IT Associate" classification. So the rule
has to be restated:

> **Your degree is worth the most where the employer is most bureaucratic.**

| Employer type | Degree value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| County · city · special district · utility · transit | **Highest** | Published classifications, degree-based MQs, scored supplementals — the same machinery as the state |
| Health systems · universities · large manufacturers | **High** | Formal Analyst I/II/III ladders; "Bachelor's in CS or related" is a literal screen you pass and most applicants don't |
| Mid-size private with a real IT department | **Moderate** | No ladder, but degree + production-floor background is an unusual combination |
| Small business · MSP · generic staffing req | **Lowest** | Scored on years of experience alone. This is where you lose to a 6-year veteran at $24/hr |

**So the non-state search is weighted deliberately toward structured employers.** That is what
"apply at your tier" means once you leave CalCareers.

The failure mode is the bottom row. Generic MSP and staffing-firm help desk reqs are the easiest to
find, the fastest to apply to, and the only ones where your degree counts for nothing. Volume there
feels like progress and isn't.

## 2. The six lanes

### Lane 1 — Local public sector (county, city, district, utility, transit)
**This is the closest thing to state service and the most under-used option you have.**

- **Titles:** IT Analyst I/II · IT Infrastructure Analyst I/II · IT Applications Analyst I/II ·
  Systems Support Specialist · IT Technician II/III · Client Services Analyst
- **Employers:** Sacramento County (in flight) · City of Sacramento · Placer County · Yolo County ·
  El Dorado County · SMUD · SacRT · Regional San · Sacramento Metro Fire
- **Portal:** GovernmentJobs.com covers most of them on one account. Set alerts once.
- **Why it works:** identical degree-MQ mechanism to the state, thinner applicant pool than private,
  single-site, and the driving requirement usually doesn't apply.
- **Watch:** hard cut-off dates, and supplemental questionnaires are *scored against the bulletin*.
  Write them in classification language, not résumé language.

### Lane 2 — Health systems
**The best long-game on this page.**

- **Titles:** Desktop Support Analyst · IT Service Desk Analyst II · Endpoint Support Analyst ·
  Clinical Systems Support · Applications Analyst I
- **Employers:** UC Davis Health · Sutter · Kaiser · Dignity/CommonSpirit · Marshall Medical
- **Why it works:** health systems carry an enormous internal analyst tier (Epic and clinical
  applications). The documented path is: get in at desktop support on one campus, then transfer
  internally. Internal transfer converts at **32× the inbound application rate** — the single
  strongest channel in the whole research file. Getting inside a large health system is the move;
  the entry title is almost irrelevant.
- **Watch:** ⚠️ no verified Sacramento pay band was confirmed for entry desktop roles — check before
  assuming. UC Davis Health is **UC, not state civil service** — separate portal, separate rules,
  and nothing to do with your CalCareers eligibility.

### Lane 3 — Education, non-state
- **Titles:** Senior IT Technician · Lab/Microcomputer Support · Ed Tech Systems Support Specialist ·
  IT User Support Technician
- **Employers:** Los Rios CCD · FCUSD · Twin Rivers · EGUSD · San Juan · SCUSD · Natomas USD · SCOE
- **Portals:** EDJOIN · schooljobs.com/careers/losriosccd · district sites directly
- **Why it works:** Los Rios is the standout — you're an SCC alum, a former STEM tutor there, *and*
  a current classified school employee. FCUSD already put you in front of a four-person panel.
  Neither is a cold application.
- **Watch:** 🚗 the licence constraint bites hardest here. Filter out anything multi-site,
  "assigned territory," or "reliable personal vehicle." Single-campus and district-office roles are
  fine.

### Lane 4 — Industrial, food-ag, manufacturing
**Your proven converter. Final two at Superior Farms came from this lane.**

- **Titles:** Manufacturing IT Support Technician · Plant IT Technician · IT/OT Technician ·
  Site IT Technician · Production Systems Technician · MES Technician
- **Employers:** Blue Diamond Growers · Nippon Shokken · Pacific Coast Producers · Sunsweet ·
  Raley's · Nugget Markets · UNFI · Sysco · plus the California League of Food Producers member
  list (clfp.com/processor-member-list)
- **Why it works:** "manufacturing/industrial experience preferred" repels the entire bootcamp and
  CS-grad pool *before you're compared to them*. Your cold-storage and food-safety background is a
  scored asset here and worth nothing anywhere else.
- **Watch:** these roles are mostly **company-direct and unindexed** — they don't reliably show up
  on Indeed. Check careers pages directly. Verify the commute before you invest.

### Lane 5 — Distribution and logistics
- **Titles:** IT Support Technician II · Deskside Support · Warehouse Systems Support · FC IT Tech
- **Employers:** Amazon · UPS · FedEx · Walmart DC · Target DC · UNFI
- **Why it works:** hires continuously, shift work is normal, and your throughput/deadline record
  reads as directly relevant. Amazon Tech II sits around **$31/hr**.
- **Watch:** the Tier 1 version of these roles pays near **$25/hr** — push for the Tech II
  classification. Check transit.

### Lane 6 — Contract-to-hire agencies
- **Firms:** Robert Half (live relationship) · Insight Global · Randstad · TEKsystems ·
  Apex Systems · Motion Recruitment
- **Why it works:** roughly **35% of temps convert to permanent**, versus ~0.5% of cold
  applications becoming hires. It is your fastest lane and your demonstrated one — Natomas came
  from here.
- **Watch:** ⚠️ **this is exactly where tier discipline breaks.** A recruiter routes you on the
  band you state. If you say "IT support," you get the $22–24/hr Tier 1 queue. Say the band out
  loud, every time, to every firm.

## 3. The 10-second screening test

Run this on a posting before you spend any time on it. Phone-friendly.

**Apply if it has ANY of these:**
- A level in the title — I/II/III, "Senior", "Tech II". A level means a real ladder and a real band.
- "Bachelor's degree required or preferred" — the degree is doing work; most applicants don't have one.
- **Analyst · Administrator · Specialist · Engineer** in the title, not just "Technician."
- A domain qualifier — manufacturing, plant, production, clinical, campus, endpoint, systems.
- A posted pay floor of **$28/hr or better**.

**Skip if:**
- "Help Desk Technician" or "Tier 1" with no level and no domain.
- Pay floor under $25/hr — that is below the Sacramento median for this work.
- "3–5 years required" with no degree-substitution clause.
- Requires a driver's licence, personal vehicle, or travel between sites — until the licence lands.
- A staffing req with no named client and no band.

> The single hardest habit to build: **an easy application is not a cheap application.** It costs
> the same attention as a good one and returns almost nothing.

## 4. Weekly operating rhythm

Built around an 8:15–2:45 shift, phone during the day, laptop at night.

| When | What | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **Mon** · phone | Portal sweep — GovernmentJobs, EDJOIN, health system careers pages. **Queue, don't apply.** | 20 min |
| **Tue** · laptop | One structured application, full supplemental, done properly | 45–60 min |
| **Wed** · phone | Two company-direct touches in the industrial lane | 15 min |
| **Thu** · laptop | One structured application + two fast ones from the queue | 60 min |
| **Fri** · phone | One agency contact, by name. Rotate firms. | 10 min |
| **Sat** | Skill-up block + one deep application | 90 min |
| **Sun** | Update the tracker, build next week's queue | 20 min |

**Volume targets — quality-capped on purpose:**
- **4–6 applications per week.** Not 20.
- **At least 3** must be structured-employer, tier-correct roles.
- **No more than 5 cold applications per week**, and only where you can attach a human name.
- **Zero** below-tier applications. This is the rule the whole plan rests on.

## 5. What to say

One sentence, used with recruiters, hiring managers, and anywhere there's a free-text field:

> *"CS degree, ITIL 4 Foundation, A+ Core 1, with hands-on enterprise device support — Windows and
> macOS imaging, deployment and break-fix at district scale, plus production-floor and cold-storage
> operations experience. I'm looking at Tier 2/3 systems and analyst-track roles, single site,
> $30+/hr, open to contract or direct."*

Three things that sentence does deliberately: it leads with the credential, it names the band, and
it puts "systems" in the recruiter's ear rather than "support."

**On the gap Brooke named.** Her feedback — *"more experience with networking and systems"* — points
at a specific bridge. Don't chase pure network engineering; that's a longer road. Chase the
**systems** half: endpoint administration, device management, imaging and deployment at fleet scale,
Jamf and Intune. That's adjacent to what you already do, it's a real title tier above technician,
and it's the shortest honest path from where you are to where the postings are.

## 6. Tracking

Every application goes in `search/applications.csv` — no exceptions, including the fast ones. The
columns that actually matter for this plan:

- **`lane`** — which of the six. At month's end, count them. If Lane 6 dominates and Lanes 1–2 are
  empty, the plan has quietly reverted to the old search.
- **`next_action` + `next_action_date`** — an application with no next action is a lottery ticket.
- **`contact`** — a human name. Applications with one convert at a different rate than applications
  without one.

**Review monthly, not weekly.** Three data points is noise. What you're looking for at 30 days:
which lanes produced a human response, not which produced the most submissions.
